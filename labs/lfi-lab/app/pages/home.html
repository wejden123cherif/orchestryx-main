Global Leaders Convene Emergency Summit on Artificial Intelligence Governance

By Margaret E. CollinsScience & Technology Correspondent

Representatives from forty-seven nations gathered in Geneva on Monday for an emergency session of the newly formed International AI Safety Council, marking what diplomats described as the most significant multilateral technology policy meeting since the establishment of internet governance protocols in the 1990s.

The summit aims to produce a binding framework for the development and deployment of advanced artificial intelligence within six months.

"We stand at an inflection point. The choices we make in the next eighteen months will define the relationship between humanity and machine intelligence for generations." — Dr. Yuki Tanaka, IASC Chair

Critics warn that any framework risks stifling innovation, while advocates argue that self-regulation has demonstrably failed to address systemic risks in prior technology cycles.
